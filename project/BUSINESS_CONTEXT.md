# Business context

## Decision and stakeholder
Governance teams migrating from CSF 1.1 to CSF 2.0 need traceable explanations of changed outcomes and potential policy ownership implications. A textual diff alone confuses renumbering with substantive change.

Users: Cybersecurity governance lead, risk owner, control owner and policy reviewer. A domain reviewer owns factual approval; the delivery lead owns release acceptance. The assistant reduces evidence assembly time while preserving human accountability.

## Before and after
Before: a reviewer assembles a narrative manually and struggles to trace individual statements. After: the reviewer receives structured candidates with original evidence, corrects uncertainty, then approves an auditable export.

## Value measurement
Use at least five comparable tasks per reviewer. Record manual baseline minutes, assisted preparation minutes, review minutes, correction count and accepted output quality. Report median end-to-end time change and the sample size. Target ≥20% reduction without lowering factual accuracy; a small sample is exploratory, not a universal productivity claim.

## Risks and limits
False equivalence, missed split/merge relationships, dynamic reference exports, overconfident impact statements and invented legal obligations. Freeze hashes and retain publication identifiers; treat organisational impact as a hypothesis.

Possible impact statements are explicitly provisional. Human judgement and source inspection remain part of the workflow.
